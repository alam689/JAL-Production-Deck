How every job runs

The same five steps for every product.

01

Check BOM & routing

What goes into one bale, level by level, and the operations that make it.

02

Run MRP

Explode the contract quantity back to raw jute, net off stock and schedule backwards from ship-by.

03

Release the stage order

One planned order per stage. Release it when material and the section are ready.

04

Post a shift entry

Input, good output, waste and downtime, every shift, checked live before it posts.

05

Complete & close

Complete when the run is done, close to freeze it with final yield and wastage.

Watched throughout on the Daily board, the Production process view and the Executive dashboard.

Quick reference

Units, checks and the order life cycle.

Order status

PlannedRelease →Releasedfirst entry posted →In progressComplete →CompletedClose →Closed

Units by family

  • Sacking & sand bags: kg → pcs at cutting, pcs → bales at packing
  • Hessian cloth: kg → yd at weaving; 40″ 10 oz = 315 g/yd
  • Geo soil saver: 553 g/yd, 75 yd per roll
  • Yarn cops: 215 g per cop, 500 per bale

Live check

  • Wastage above the stage alert level turns amber (batching 3%, weaving 2.5%, sewing 0.8%)
  • Unaccounted above 3%: waste not weighed or input mis-recorded
  • Red = cannot post: output + waste > input + 2%, duplicate shift, missing downtime reason, future date

Common questions

  • Night shift C belongs to the date it starts
  • Posted entries cannot be edited — use Save draft if unsure
  • Order missing from shift entry? It must be Released or In progress
  • Stages fully covered by stock get no order

Prototype v0.1

What the prototype does not do yet.

01

Stock is not reserved

MRP runs do not reduce stock; the full ERP reserves stock per contract.

02

No stage sequencing

Sewing could post before cutting had produced; the full ERP issues from the previous stage’s lots.

03

No lot tracking

Which raw-jute lot went into which bale is planned in SRS FR-INV-013.

04

Illustrative data

Machine status, export orders, FG stock, attendance and BOM quantities are demo figures.
